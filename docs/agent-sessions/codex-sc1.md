CORE DESIGN (Nicoh's intent — follow closely):
- Everyday moral grey areas, not melodrama. EVERY real decision is a grey-area dilemma. There is NO score, NO game over, NO explicit reward/penalty, no numbers for morality. Consequences are real but quiet and delayed: shown through narrative and environment, so the player becomes acutely aware of the costs of accumulating small compromises "just to survive".
- Day structure (keep the slice-of-life pacing; no 7-day 'run' gimmick): Days continue indefinitely. Each day: MORNING at home with Laura (1 dilemma), the CART SHIFT (coffee-serving minigame + 2–3 dilemmas interleaved: regulars, the rival cart across the street, supplier, permit officer), EVENING (1 dilemma: Laura, bills, the custody social worker, the landlord), then a short NIGHT JOURNAL written in Melanie's voice reflecting the day's choices without judgment. Every 5 days a quiet "chapter" vignette about how Laura is growing (e.g., she learned to make her own lunch; she stopped telling Melanie about school; or she made Melanie a drawing of the cart), then play continues.
- Hidden state (never shown as numbers): money (shown only as cash in the register/coins in a jar, can run short), lauraCloseness, lauraSelfReliance (growing up alone), lauraTrust, melanieFatigue, melanieNormalization (how used to compromises she is), regularsTrust per regular, rivalRelation, and a compromise ledger (list of what was done). These drive: which dilemma variants appear, Laura's lines and fridge drawings, regulars' behavior, journal text, and a subtle visual palette (the grayscale world gains/loses a warm accent color).
- Slippery slope: some temptations recur in escalating forms (e.g., shortchanging, watering milk, taking from the tip jar). After the player has done a thing once, the next variant's wording treats it as normal ("like last time"), so the player notices drift.
- Callbacks: past choices resurface days later (a shortchanged student can no longer afford coffee; the rival remembers who helped; Laura mentions the missed recital).
- Regulars with hidden lives (5): revealed gradually across days through small talk and dilemmas (e.g., the overtipping businessman is secretly laid off; the quiet student pays in coins; the nurse after night shift; the elderly man who orders for his late wife; the beat cop who lets things slide). Rival cart owner across the street (give him a name and his own struggles) — cooperation vs competition dilemmas.
- Seed ~24 dilemmas now as DATA (JSON/TS) with fields: id, title, moment (morning/shift/evening), conditions (state predicates, prior choices), situation text, 2–3 options each with hidden effects + ledger entry + optional callback scheduling + journal line, and escalation chain id. Claude will add more research-based dilemmas later through the same format, so make adding dilemmas data-only and validated by tests. Include examples: shortchange a distracted customer; keep extra change a regular forgot; water down milk to stretch supply; let Laura's call go to voicemail during a rush; tell the rival the health inspector is coming (a lie that helps you); take a box of the rival's cups that fell off his truck; take medicine from a pharmacy for feverish Laura when cash is gone; sell yesterday's milk; take cash-only to dodge the permit fee; tell the social worker Laura is never home alone; leave Laura alone in the apartment to cover an early shift; birthday gift vs permit renewal; accept a "favor" from a regular you suspect; report the rival's expired permit.

MINIGAME (active, skillful, short): serving coffee. Customers queue with patience bars (portraits). An order shows a recipe as a short key sequence the player types quickly (e.g., G-T-P-S-L for grind/tamp/pull/steam/lid shown as big pixel key caps; drinks vary), plus quick small-talk lines with regulars (pick one of two replies, which can reveal their hidden lives). Mistakes cost time; tired Melanie (high fatigue) gets occasional "shaky hands" (a key swaps), so the theme lives in the mechanic. Tips vary. A shift lasts ~90–120 seconds. Make it feel good: crisp key-cap animations, steam, coin clink (optional WebAudio blips), customer reactions.
