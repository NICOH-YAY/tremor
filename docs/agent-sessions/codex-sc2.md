# Research-grounded dilemma cards for "Small Change" (add via the dilemma data format)
Citations were recalled by research agents WITHOUT live verification: put them in docs/DILEMMA-SOURCES.md marked "citation to verify"; never present them as verified. Treat ego depletion (Gino 2011) and the morning-morality effect (Kouchaki & Smith 2014) as flavor only (shaky replication).

## Design rules from the research
- Slippery slope (Welsh, Ordonez et al. 2015, J Applied Psych 100(1), doi:10.1037/a0036681): recurring temptations escalate in SMALL steps. Chain "stock": stretch milk one day -> skip the temperature log "just today" -> sell yesterday's pastries as fresh ("rotate stock") -> relabel date stickers. Hidden counter of accepted steps. After each accepted step: offer the compromise first, honest option last; narration drifts to euphemisms (Bandura's moral disengagement). For players who refused early, keep the honest option phrased plainly so the contrast exists. Late callback: "she had stopped checking." No judgment, no score.
- A second chain "till": give exact change -> "miscount" a dollar -> round the tip jar up -> skim on slow days.
- Fatigue framing: the SAME temptation appears in the morning (easier to refuse) and evening (phrased with more pressure) so players can notice their own pattern.
- Hurry (Darley & Batson 1973, doi:10.1037/h0034449): when Melanie is late for Laura's pickup, helping options visibly cost time; the game never comments.
- Tag each card with 1-2 moral foundations (care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity, liberty; Graham et al. 2011, doi:10.1037/a0021847) so the set spans more than care vs fairness.

## Cards (title | moment | situation | options | quiet echo | concept)
1. Short Change | shift | A distracted customer thinks they paid with a ten but handed a twenty. | give full change / give what they expect / miscount by a dollar or two | weeks later Melanie rounds her own tip jar up without noticing | fudge factor (Mazar, Amir & Ariely 2008, doi:10.1509/jmkr.45.6.633)
2. Laura's Form | evening after a bad rush | School form asks custody status; the truth needs a long explanation. | explain honestly / tick the easier box / leave blank | a teacher's note comes home phrased carefully; Laura asks why "separated" isn't on her form | depletion (flavor)
3. Their Fault | shift | The rival's permit is a week late and the inspector is on the block. | say nothing / tip the inspector casually / mention it to a gossipy regular | Melanie later says "he'd do it to me" unprompted | moral disengagement (Bandura 1999, doi:10.1207/s15327957pspr0303_3)
4. Cash Only | morning | Landlord offers lower rent for cash, no receipt, "saves us both paperwork." | take it / insist on a receipt / ask for a written lease | the social worker later asks for income documents and the gap shows | ethical fading (Tenbrunsel & Messick 2004)
5. Earned It | closing | She gave a free coffee to a man who could not pay; the till is $6 short. | cover from her wallet / call it even / skim a bit more, the day was generous | he returns with thanks and Melanie cannot meet his eye | moral licensing (Merritt, Effron & Monin 2010)
6. Tunnel | rent week | A cheap pallet of beans on a short deadline means missing Laura's school event. | take the deal / ask the supplier to hold / send a neighbor to the event | Laura draws the family without Melanie | scarcity (Shah, Mullainathan & Shafir 2012, Science)
7. Late Delivery | evening | The tired supplier over-delivered by a few units and has not noticed. | point it out / accept silently / ask for a credit slip | the same temptation reappears in a morning scene | fatigue (flavor)
8. Everyone Saw | shift | A regular drops a folded paper (an eviction notice) in a crowded line. | return it now / wait till the line thins / let it lie | a bystander later refuses Melanie a favor: "somebody else will" | diffusion of responsibility (Darley & Latane 1968)
9. Free Pastry | morning | A friendly court clerk who brings pastries asks her to hold a sealed envelope "for a friend." | take it / refuse politely / take it and tell the social worker | the clerk appears at the custody hearing, friendly; nothing is said | reciprocity (Gouldner 1960)
10. By the Book | shift | An inspector hints he will overlook a missing sticker if he can skip the line every morning. | comply / refuse / ask for the rule in writing | his visits grow casual; others start to expect the same | authority (Milgram 1963)
11. Good Coffee | afternoon | A regular who just lost his job asks if his resume is good. It is not. | say it is great / gentle honest feedback / joke it off | rejection -> "you told me it was fine" OR he returns a week later with a revised one | prosocial lies (Levine & Schweitzer 2014)
12. Late Rent Grace | rent day | Landlord will let rent slide if Melanie sells to his cousin's crew at a discount every week. | take the deal / pay and skip the restock / ask for a written extension | the "extension" is never written and returns in the next dispute; Laura asks why they whisper in the hallway | eviction (Desmond, Evicted)
13. Cash Float | weekend | A regular offers $60 cash for a week of unlicensed home-kitchen catering. | do it / decline / do half | the apartment smells of prep; Laura stops inviting classmates | informal economy ($2.00 a Day, Edin & Shaefer)
14. The Early Pickup | morning | Supplier offers a 5am pickup at a better price; school drop-off is 8:15. | take it, leave Laura asleep alone / pay the higher price | Laura sets her own alarm and says "I'm fine" | time poverty (Ehrenreich; Mullainathan & Shafir)
15. His Weekend | evening | Her ex offers to take Laura for the weekend if Melanie drops her claim on his debts. | accept / refuse | Laura packs her own bag with a drawing for each parent | relationships (Edin & Kefalas, Promises I Can Keep)
16. Parent Night | evening | Parent night is the best sales evening of the week. | go / stay open / ask a neighbor to cover | Laura stops handing over school notices | time poverty
17. Cart Corner | rainy afternoon | Laura reads at the cart; a customer says Melanie should get a babysitter. | let her stay / send her to a neighbor / close early | the next week Laura counts the till without being asked | parentification (Jurkovic 1997); childcare deserts
18. Rival's Spot | lunch | The rival's cart is unattended while a permit inspector walks the block. | warn him / say nothing / point the inspector his way | he later undercuts her, or covers for her in a pinch | sidewalk norms (Duneier, Sidewalk)
19. Social Worker Visit | evening before | The visit is tomorrow; Laura's room is messy and the fridge half empty. | stage the apartment / show it honestly / reschedule | Laura asks if "the lady" is checking if she is good | institutions and class (Lareau, Unequal Childhoods)
20. Reporting Tips | evening | Tips were about $200 and the benefits form is due. | report all / report some / cut hours | Laura's school-lunch status changes | benefit cliffs (Edin & Lein, Making Ends Meet)
21. Dinner at the Cart | closing | Day-old pastries are free but the cooler broke. | eat them / throw them out / serve them tomorrow | Laura learns "the cart food is dinner" | food insecurity, sanctity
22. The Pantry Fib | closing | A shop's unsold fruit sits out; Melanie could take a handful. | take it / pay / ask | Laura asks what "borrowing" means | necessity theft
23. Medicine | night (only when cash is short and Laura is sick) | Laura has a fever; the pharmacy line is long and the register is unattended. | wait and pay with rent money / take it / go home and hope | shown only through Laura's recovery and Melanie's journal | necessity theft

## Laura growth moments (no verdict, chosen by hidden state)
- She makes her own lunch and leaves Melanie's untouched.
- She stops saying "guess what" after school.
- She keeps a spare key on a string and stops asking when Melanie will be home.
- She draws two houses; later, only one.
- She apologizes too much for spilling a drink and stops asking to bake together.
- She waits by the door with the cash box ready: "Did we make enough?"
Warm counterparts when closeness is high: she draws the cart with both of them in it; she saves half her cookie for Melanie; she tells a long story about recess.
